Northstar Operations Group Employee Experience and Wellbeing Policy

Purpose

This policy establishes practices intended to support employee satisfaction, effective management, sustainable workloads, professional development, recognition, and a healthy work experience.

The policy is designed to provide consistent expectations while allowing departments to adapt practices to role requirements, customer commitments, security needs, and operational realities.

Employee Feedback

Northstar conducts a quarterly employee pulse survey covering overall satisfaction, manager effectiveness, workload balance, learning access, recognition, confidence in leadership, clarity of priorities, and intent to remain with the organization.

Survey results are reviewed by department leaders and summarized for employees. Leaders should communicate the major themes, actions being considered, accountable owners, and expected timing.

Survey results are directional indicators. They should be evaluated alongside retention, absenteeism, staffing levels, workload, delivery performance, quality, and business results. Survey results alone do not prove that a policy or manager caused a particular outcome.

Manager Practices

People managers should hold a meaningful one-on-one conversation with each direct report at least monthly. Conversations should cover priorities, workload, development goals, feedback, recognition, career interests, wellbeing concerns, and barriers to effective work.

Managers should document agreed actions without recording unnecessary personal or sensitive information. Employees should have a way to raise concerns when a one-on-one is missed or does not provide a meaningful opportunity for discussion.

Workload and Capacity

Managers must review workload when an employee reports sustained over-capacity work, missed recovery time, recurring priority conflicts, excessive after-hours work, or inability to meet commitments despite reasonable effort.

Possible responses include rebalancing work, adjusting deadlines, clarifying priorities, reducing nonessential activities, adding resources, changing staffing, escalating delivery risks, or arranging additional support.

Repeated capacity problems should be reviewed at the team or department level rather than treated only as individual performance issues.

Flexible Work

Eligible teams may use a flexible work model based on role requirements, client commitments, collaboration needs, security considerations, service quality, and team coordination.

Flexible work arrangements should be reviewed when service quality, responsiveness, collaboration, workload balance, or customer outcomes decline. Decisions should be communicated clearly and applied consistently within comparable roles.

Learning and Development

Employees should receive access to at least four hours of structured learning time per month when business commitments permit. Learning may include technical training, professional skills, required compliance training, mentoring, communities of practice, or role-specific development.

Managers should discuss development goals at least quarterly and connect learning activities to current responsibilities and future capability needs.

Recognition

Managers are encouraged to recognize meaningful contributions, including customer impact, teamwork, quality improvements, innovation, risk reduction, knowledge sharing, and support for colleagues.

Recognition should not reward unsustainable working hours or encourage employees to bypass required controls.

Escalation and Support

Employees should raise workload, conduct, wellbeing, or management concerns through their manager, skip-level leader, Human Resources, or another approved support channel. Concerns involving safety, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or serious misconduct should use the appropriate formal reporting process.

Employee Metrics

Leadership may review satisfaction, manager effectiveness, workload balance, learning access, one-on-one completion, learning-time completion, voluntary attrition, absence patterns, internal mobility, and staffing levels.

Metrics should be interpreted carefully. A high survey score does not prove that a policy is effective in every context, and a low score does not identify a single cause. Leaders should compare trends over time, departments, roles, and relevant business conditions.

Policy Review

The policy should be reviewed annually and when there are significant changes to work arrangements, staffing, business strategy, security requirements, or employee feedback. Improvement actions should have an owner, target date, and method for assessing progress.
